## **Registered Jack (RJ) Connectors**

### **RJ11 (Registered Jack type 11)**

The RJ11 is a common connector used primarily for **analog telephony** and **DSL** connections, as the latter utilize the same telephone cabling.

- **Structure:** It is a **6-position** connector, but typically only **2 conductors** are used internally. For this reason, it is often identified by the designation **6P2C** (6 position, 2 conductor).
- **Dimensions:** Compared to the Ethernet standard, the RJ11 is smaller and narrower.

### **RJ45 (Registered Jack type 45)**

The RJ45 is the standard connector for **Ethernet** connections and LANs.

- **Structure:** Unlike the RJ11, this connector has **8 positions and 8 internal conductors** (8P8C), utilizing all of them for data transmission.
- **Comparison:** Visually, it is very similar to the RJ11 but is **larger and wider** to accommodate the greater number of pins.

## **Coaxial Cable Connectors**

### **F-Connector**

This connector is primarily used to connect **cable modems** to cable television infrastructures.

- **Characteristics:** It features an **internal thread** that allows it to be screwed securely onto the device port, ensuring a reliable connection.
- **Standard:** It is often associated with the **DOCSIS** (_Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification_) standard, which is the standard for transporting data over cable networks. The signal is carried by the single copper conductor located at the center of the coaxial cable.

### **BNC Connector (Bayonet Neill-Concelman)**

The BNC connector is another type of coaxial cable interface, often used for **WAN** connections or other types of video and data links.

- **Locking mechanism:** The "B" in the name stands for **Bayonet**; the connector is pushed in and then given a slight twist to lock it into place. This mechanism prevents the cable from being accidentally disconnected, requiring a reverse twist for removal.
- **Origin of the name:** The letters "NC" are derived from the surnames of its creators, Paul Neill of Bell Labs and Carl Concelman of Amphenol.

---

**Study Tip:** Remember that while RJ connectors are transparent and show the individual colored wires, coaxial connectors (F and BNC) are typically metallic and designed for environments that require greater shielding or superior mechanical retention.
