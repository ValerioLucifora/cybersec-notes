## Introduction and Advantages of Fiber Connectivity

Optical fiber represents an advanced alternative to traditional twisted‑pair copper, enabling data transmission via **light** rather than electrical signals. One of the primary advantages lies in **security**: unlike copper, which emits radio frequencies (RF) and is relatively easy to tap, it is extremely difficult to "breach" a fiber connection without the system detecting it.

Furthermore, fiber excels over **long distances**, since the light signal does not degrade as quickly as radio frequencies do; if copper is limited to about 100 meters, fiber can transmit data for kilometers. Finally, fiber is completely **immune to radio frequency interference (RFI)**, making it ideal for industrial or work environments subject to electromagnetic disturbances.

## Physical Cable Structure and Components

To understand how fiber works, it is useful to examine its internal structure:

- **Source and Receiver:** The signal originates from a light source (**LED or laser**) and is captured by a receiver at the other end.
- **Core:** This is the central part of the cable, highly reflective, where the light travels by bouncing from side to side.
- **Cladding:** A low‑reflection layer that surrounds the core to keep the light contained within it.
- **Buffer Coating:** An outer protective layer that envelops the entire structure to preserve its integrity.
- **Connector and Ferrule:** At the cable's end, a connector uses a protective **ferrule** to surround and precisely align the fiber core.

## Types of Optical Fiber

There are two main variants of fiber optic cables, chosen based on specific reach and cost requirements:

### Multimode Fiber

Multimode fiber is commonly used for **short‑range** communications, with distances typically reaching up to **2 kilometers**. It uses less expensive light sources, such as **LEDs**. Its **core is relatively large** (larger than the wavelength of the light), which allows the light to propagate along multiple paths (or "modes") by bouncing off the inner walls of the cable.

### Single‑mode Fiber

This type is designed to cover much longer distances; some standards can transmit data up to **100 kilometers** without needing to regenerate the signal. To achieve such performance, very intense light sources such as **lasers** or high‑power LEDs are used. Single‑mode fiber has a **very small core**, which allows light to pass through a single path (single mode), minimizing signal dispersion.
